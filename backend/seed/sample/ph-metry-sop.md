# pH Metry - Standard Operating Procedure (project working copy)

## 1. Purpose
This procedure describes how to calibrate a pH meter and measure the pH of aqueous solutions so that results are accurate and reproducible. It is the working copy used by the pH Metry Method Validation project.

## 2. Scope
It applies to benchtop pH meters with a combined glass electrode and automatic temperature compensation (ATC) used for buffers, titrations and routine sample measurements between pH 2 and pH 12.

## 3. Equipment and materials
pH meter with combined glass pH electrode and ATC probe. Magnetic stirrer and stir bar. Certified buffer solutions pH 4.01, pH 7.00 and pH 10.01 at 25 C. Electrode storage solution, 3 M KCl. Deionised water in a wash bottle. Lint-free tissue. Calibrated thermometer.

## 4. Preparation
Check the expiry date of every buffer before use; an expired buffer must never be used for calibration. Pour fresh portions of buffer into clean beakers and allow them to reach room temperature, 25 C plus or minus 2 C. Inspect the electrode for cracks, air bubbles in the bulb and a dry junction. Rinse the electrode with deionised water and blot it gently; never wipe the glass bulb.

## 5. Calibration
Calibrate at the start of each working day and after any electrode cleaning or replacement. Use a three-point calibration with pH 7.00 first, then pH 4.01 and pH 10.01. Stir gently and wait until the stable reading indicator appears, normally within 30 seconds. Rinse the electrode with deionised water between buffers. After the third buffer record the slope and the offset shown by the meter.

## 6. Calibration acceptance criteria
The electrode slope must be between 95 percent and 105 percent. The offset at pH 7.00 must be within plus or minus 30 mV. The response time to a stable reading must not exceed 30 seconds. If any criterion is not met do not measure samples; clean the electrode, repeat the calibration with fresh buffers, and replace the electrode if the result is still outside the limits.

## 7. Sample measurement
Rinse the electrode with deionised water and then with a small portion of the sample. Immerse the electrode so that the junction is fully covered and stir gently. Record the pH when the stable reading indicator appears together with the sample temperature. Rinse the electrode between samples. Check the calibration at the end of a series by measuring the pH 7.00 buffer; the reading must be within 0.05 pH units of the nominal value.

## 8. Temperature effects
The pH of many buffers changes with temperature. Tris buffer changes by about minus 0.03 pH units for every degree Celsius increase. Always record the temperature next to the pH value and report pH at the stated temperature.

## 9. Electrode care and storage
Store the electrode in 3 M KCl storage solution and never in deionised water. Clean a sluggish electrode by soaking it in 0.1 M HCl for 15 minutes and then rinsing it well. An electrode whose slope remains below 95 percent after cleaning is at the end of its life and must be replaced.

## 10. Troubleshooting
Slope below 95 percent: clean or replace the electrode and check the buffers for expiry. Slow or drifting readings: check for a blocked junction, a dry electrode or air bubbles in the bulb. Unstable readings in low ionic strength samples: use a slower stirring rate. Offset outside the limit: repeat the calibration with fresh pH 7.00 buffer.

## 11. Records
Record the date, analyst, electrode identifier, buffer lot numbers and expiry dates, slope, offset, temperature and every measured pH value in the experiment record.
